Now that all the pieces are on the table, the final message becomes clear.

The question is not only:

Which model is stronger?

The deeper question is:

How do we represent images in the first place?

We started from a prompt.

Then we moved through co-embedding spaces, visual words, codebooks, Transformers, diffusion, guidance, latent spaces, and efficient inference.

The Text-to-Pixels journey is not one magical box.

It is a machine made of many layers.

And the first big lesson is this:

Image representation is the key.

If we represent images as raw pixels, the model has to work in a massive space.

Every detail is explicit.

Every color value is there.

Every denoising step is expensive.

But if we represent images as latent vectors or discrete tokens, we compress the important information.

We give the model a smaller, more structured space to work in.

That is why representation keeps appearing again and again in this story.

CLIP represents text and images in a shared embedding space.

VQGAN represents images as discrete tokens.

Latent diffusion represents images as compressed latent vectors.

VAR represents image generation as a sequence of scales.

Different representations open different doors.

The second lesson is that architecture still matters.

Diffusion models can be effective with U-Nets.

They can also be pushed toward Transformer-based architectures.

But architecture and representation work together.

A powerful model in a bad space may still be inefficient.

A smart representation can make the model’s job much easier.

The third lesson is more uncomfortable.

Beautiful generations are not free.

They come from larger datasets, more compute, and increasingly expensive training.

The slide reminds us of the scale jump from ImageNet, with about 1.2 million images, to LAION, with billions of images.

And behind those images are many GPU and TPU hours.

A lot of electricity.

A lot of hardware.

And probably at least one engineer staring at a training dashboard at 3 a.m. whispering,

“Please do not crash.”

Training is expensive.

But inference is the cost that repeats.

Every time a user types a prompt, the system has to generate again.

If millions of users do this, inference cost becomes a central problem.

That is why methods like MarkovGen, LatentCRF, SANA, and VAR are not small details.

They are part of making text-to-image generation practical.

Faster.

Cheaper.

More scalable.

So, Text-to-Pixels is not a single invention.

It is a convergence.

Alignment.

Tokenization.

Denoising.

Guidance.

Compression.

Structured inference.

Efficient generation.

From text to vectors.

From vectors to tokens or latents.

From noise to structure.

From structure to pixels.

And from pixels, the road now expands toward video, 3D, and interactive worlds.

The journey from language to images is already impressive.

But it is also only the beginning.
