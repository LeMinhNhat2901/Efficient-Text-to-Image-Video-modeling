Today, a single line of text can generate a robot cooking in a kitchen, a robot painted as graffiti on a brick wall, a raccoon wearing formal clothes and a tophat, or even a hyper-realistic alien pyramid landscape.

But the story does not stop at static images. Text-to-image becomes one of the building blocks for text-to-video: generate keyframes, then refine motion with temporal and spatial super-resolution.

And with the right 3D representation, text-to-image can also support text-to-3D systems, where image generation and NeRF-like representations work together to construct objects in space.

Behind these systems, most modern conditional generation pipelines rely heavily on two families of methods.

The first is autoregressive generation: build the output step by step, token by token, much like language models predict the next word.

The second is diffusion-based generation: start from noise and gradually recover structure through a learned denoising process.

This is why text-to-image is not just one application. It is a centerpiece. Text-to-video can use text-to-image models to generate keyframes. Text-to-3D can use text-to-image and NeRF models as building blocks. Once we can map language to images, we can extend the same idea to time, space, and interaction.
