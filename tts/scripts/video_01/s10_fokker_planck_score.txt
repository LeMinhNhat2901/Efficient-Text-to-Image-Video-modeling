The answer is the Fokker-Planck equation.

SDEs describe how individual samples move.

Fokker-Planck describes how the whole probability density evolves.

In the animation, the drift term and the diffusion term are assembled into a single equation.

The density behaves like a fluid moving under a velocity field v:

partial p over partial t,

equals negative divergence of p times v.

This means that probability mass is flowing through space.

But now look at the velocity field:

v of x and t,

equals alpha of x and t,

minus beta of t over two,

times nabla log p of x and t.

And there it is again.

The score.

nabla log p of x and t.

The same score compass from the discrete story returns inside the continuous probability flow.

This is the bridge between the two worlds.

In discrete time, the score helped us step from noisy points toward higher probability.

In continuous time, the score becomes part of the velocity field that moves the entire probability density.

So Fokker-Planck is not just a terrifying equation with divergence symbols trying to ruin your afternoon.

It is the law of motion for probability itself.

It tells us how the cloud stretches, compresses, diffuses, and flows.

And if probability can flow forward, then naturally, we ask:

Can it flow backward?
